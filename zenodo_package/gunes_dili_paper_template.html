1. Introduction: The Statistical Pathology of Subword Tokenization

In modern statistical language modeling and deep neural network NLP, tokenization constitutes the foundational interface between continuous mathematical representations and discrete symbolic human language. Current dominant tokenization algorithms—primarily Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) (Sennrich et al., 2016), WordPiece, and SentencePiece/Unigram (Kudo, 2018)—operate purely on statistical surface substring frequencies. Consequently, they remain entirely agnostic to underlying morphological hierarchies, semantic roots, and phonological invariants.

This statistical detachment creates severe empirical and theoretical pathologies:

  • Morphological Boundary Fragmentation: Meaningful stems are frequently splintered into non-semantic subwords, obscuring morphological derivation in agglutinative languages (e.g., Turkish, Finnish, Hungarian).
  • Adversarial Fragility and Hallucination Entropy: Minor suffix variations or unseen neologisms cause erratic subword splits, leading directly to model hallucination and syntactic breakdown.
  • Memory-Wall and Latency Bottlenecks: Dynamic vocabulary embedding lookups and cross-entropy projection layers consume gigabytes of high-bandwidth VRAM, imposing severe latency penalties during edge and real-time neuromorphic execution.

To overcome these structural limitations, we propose a return to first principles by formalizing the core intuitions of the Güneş Dil Teorisi (Sun Language Theory) formulated in 1935 (Koşay, 1936; TDK, 1936). Historically conceived as an inquiry into the phonosemantic origins of human speech centered around solar observation and primal vocalization, the hypothesis posited that human language evolves from an invariant, atomized matrix of phonemic units possessing intrinsic physical and sensory attributes. In this work, we reconstruct this hypothesis as a rigorous mathematical, algorithmic, and neuromorphic architecture.
